"How long will it take?" is usually the first question leadership asks, and most public answers are vague. Here is what published examples actually show, and why they differ so much.

## What public examples show

| Institution | Starting point | Reported timeline | Scale noted |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Barton Community College](https://www.casestudies.com/company/ellucian/case-study/moving-to-cloud-in-the-midst-of-a-crisis) | Cloud migration with Banner, Ellucian Cloud and Degree Works | 10 months, live 23 March 2020 | Single college |
| [Broward College](https://www.sigcorp.com/case-studies/modernizing-erp-systems-with-strategic-guidance-from-sig-on-banner-saas/) | About 30-year homegrown system | Staggered go-live from Fall 2025, full launch planned for Fall 2026 | 100-person team, 88 million records, 75+ integrations |
| [UNC Greensboro](https://its.uncg.edu/tech-initiatives/ellucian-saas-modernization/) | Existing Banner institution | "Over the next several years"; no detailed timeline set | Planning sessions scheduled for October 2026 |

Three examples cannot give you a benchmark. They are also not like-for-like: Barton's project predates today's packaging, Broward's outcomes are still marked "in progress," and two of the three accounts come from a vendor or its partner.

They do show why a single number is misleading. A college moving an existing system and a large institution replacing a decades-old platform are very different projects.

## What drives your timeline

| Driver | Why it matters | What to count |
|---|---|---|
| **Starting point** | Existing Banner sites start from known processes; a move from another system usually means more redesign | Current system and version |
| **Integrations** | Each one may need [rebuilding on supported tools](/blog/banner-saas-direct-database-access) | Every feed, script and connection |
| **Customisation** | Each item must be retired, replaced or redesigned | Mods, PL/SQL, custom jobs |
| **Data** | Older data takes more conversion and validation | Years of data, known quality issues |
| **Reporting** | Direct-database reports need a supported route | Reports actually in use |
| **People** | Staff still have live terms to run | Named people and time available |
| **Calendar** | Few windows are safe for cutover | Peak dates over the next two years |

Broward's data is a good illustration: its case study mentions 88 million records dating back to the 1960s, which needed validation support from the project team.

## The people risk is real

Broward's case study names "staff hesitancy and cultural resistance" and the "risk of burnout" across its project team as challenges. Its partner's response included rebalancing work and phasing configuration and training.

Barton credits simpler disciplines: a solid project plan, weekly stakeholder meetings and detailed issue tracking.

Those points apply whatever your size. Your Banner experts cannot run a live term and a migration at full capacity, so plan for backfill.

## How to estimate your own timeline

1. **Complete the inventory first.** Any timeline without one is a guess. Our [five-phase migration plan](/blog/banner-saas-migration-plan) shows how to build it.
2. **Mark every immovable date.** Include registration, fee assessment, grade roll, payroll year-end, aid year rollover and statutory returns for two years.
3. **Pick your go-live window and work backwards.** Allow for at least two full rehearsals.
4. **Build in a fallback.** Know the next safe window if a rehearsal fails, and what that delay would cost.
5. **Test the estimate with a peer.** Ask Ellucian for a reference with a similar starting point, then ask that institution what took longer than planned.

## Do you have to decide now?
